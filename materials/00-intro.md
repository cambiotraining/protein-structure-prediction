## Course Overview

Understanding how proteins fold into their functional three-dimensional structure is a cornerstone of modern molecular biology. This course offers a deep dive into the principles, methods, and tools used to model three-dimensional protein structures, with a focus on both experimental techniques and state-of-the-art computational approaches.

### Why study protein structure modelling?

Proteins are the workhorses of the body cells. They catalyse different enzymatic reactions, transmit signals, provide structural support among other abundant tasks we could mention. These underlying functions are intricately tied to their 3D shapes. Nevertheless, despite the vast abundance of genomic data available, a mojor challenge (now partly solved!) in biology remains is: predicting or determining what a given protein looks like from amino acids sequence.

The course addresses the challenge by exploring:

- The science of protein folding and its relevance to health, disease, and bioitechnology

- De novo structure prediction, especially recent breakthroughs enabled by advances in Artificail Intelligence (AI) such as AlphaFold

- The advantages and limitations of experimental methods including X-ray crystallography, cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM), and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR)

- How these experimental methods form the basis of training computational methods, and when and how to rely on one method over another.

#### Learning Objectives

- The theoretical foundations of protein structure and folding.

- How to select and apply appropriate structure prediction tools.

- Best prectices for critically analysing and validating predictred structures.

- How to visualise models including protein-ligand interactions with modern software like ChimeraX

- How experimental data (e.g. from the Protein Data Bank) supports and shapes computational prediction.
